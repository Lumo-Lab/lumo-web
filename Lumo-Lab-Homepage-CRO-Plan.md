# Lumo Lab — Homepage Conversion Optimization Plan

**Objective:** turn qualified US traffic into conversations. Not a visual redesign — a messaging, trust, and CTA overhaul that keeps the current clean, premium, minimal aesthetic.

**Core principle:** every section does one job and moves the visitor one step closer to contacting us. If a section doesn't reduce uncertainty or increase trust, it gets cut.

**A note on numbers:** everything marked `[VERIFY]` is a placeholder — replace with a real, defensible figure before publishing. Never ship an unverified number. Everything *not* marked is already true and live in your own content (Clutch 5.0, Noctrix→ResMed $340M, Farmwave 2025 award, Nomo in all 50 states, Muvr→Exactech, 20+ partners since 2017).

## 8. "How it starts" — risk reduction before the ask

A short 3-step band that answers "what actually happens if I contact you?" This is the single most effective addition for a hesitant CTO.

1. **You send context** — the idea, current situation, timeline, constraints. Five minutes, in writing.
2. **A senior engineer reviews it** — not a coordinator. You get an honest read on scope, risk, and the smartest first step — within one business day.
3. **You decide** — a plan, an estimate, or a call. No deck, no pressure, no obligation.

**CTA:** `Send your project brief` → `/contact`

**Why:** uncertainty about the *process* is a silent conversion killer. Spelling out a fast, low-commitment, no-pressure path makes reaching out feel safe.

## 10. UX recommendations per section

- **Hero:** headline ≥ everything. One primary button (solid), one secondary (outline). Keep the reassurance micro-line. Outcomes panel stays restrained (no big icons).
- **Trust bar:** thin, quiet, linked. Never animate it or auto-scroll it (you already removed the marquee — good).
- **Self-selection:** clean rows, not a card grid. On mobile, stack title → description → CTA.
- **Flagship cases:** three static cards, consistent image ratio and height, whole card clickable, visible "View case study" on *every* card, subtle hover only, keyboard focusable. No hover-only content.
- **Services:** outcome-first text, no badge wall, one "Learn more" affordance per item.
- **Founder:** real portrait, lots of whitespace, first-person voice, single CTA.
- **Process:** 3 steps, horizontal on desktop, stacked on mobile, no timeline theatrics.
- **Testimonials:** all visible (no carousel), named + role + company, link to the related case where one exists.
- **Global:** consistent button hierarchy (primary solid / secondary outline / tertiary text+arrow), one primary per screen, visible focus states, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, no popups, no sticky mobile CTA.

## 11. Conversion rationale (why each change lifts inquiries)

- **Outcome-led hero + subhead** — visitors decide to stay or leave in seconds; naming *who we help* and *what outcome* keeps the qualified ones reading.
- **Lower-friction CTAs** — "Ask a senior engineer" / "Get a free architecture review" convert far better than "Book an assessment" because they read as advice, not procurement. More top-of-funnel asks = more leads.
- **Immediate trust bar** — resolves the "can a small Croatian team ship serious products?" doubt with an acquisition, an FDA-cleared device, a nationwide rollout, and a 5.0 Clutch — before hesitation forms.
- **Self-selection** — lets each visitor type recognize themselves and enter a context-matched funnel; relevance raises intent.
- **Outcomes before services** — buyers trust results before descriptions; leading with proof earns the right to describe services.
- **Founder section** — kills the two biggest agency fears (juniors, salespeople). Founder-led is your durable differentiator; make it explicit.
- **"How it starts"** — removing process uncertainty makes the click feel safe.
- **Context-preserving contact flow** (`need`/`source`) — continuity from homepage promise to contact form reduces drop-off at the last step.
- **Calculator as a fallback CTA** — captures budget-curious visitors who won't yet talk, instead of losing them.
